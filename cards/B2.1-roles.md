## Summary
- What is wanted: ONE role registry mapping `designer`, `implementer`,
  `verifier`, `qa`, `advisor`, `judge`, `mechanical` to configurable model
  bindings, plus the resolver that turns a role and requested effort into a
  dispatch. `designer` is also the coordinator's reasoning tier. A second
  implementer binding may be selected explicitly by ref. `verifier` and
  `qa` are pinned to maximum effort; a lower request is raised.
- Why it matters: doubt and QA must not be under-powered or collapsed into
  whatever model the coordinator happens to run. One registry prevents the
  control surface and the router from drifting into different role maps.

## Context
- Roles and duties (pinned): `designer` owns diagnosis, cards, orchestration
  and final review, never implementation; `implementer` owns repository
  changes; `verifier` owns card-check, red confirmation, verification,
  `/verify`, `/synchronize`, audits and cheap first-pass review; `qa` owns
  `/qa` and bug scans at max, dispatched by its own ref and never through
  the generic issue router; `advisor` gives optional second opinions and
  never verdicts; `judge` is read-only and enters only on B3.2's triggers;
  `mechanical` owns watchers, cleanup, bookkeeping and bounded integration
  chores, never a hand-written edit.
- Bindings are configuration, not hardcoded model names. A desk may map, for
  example, designer/coordinator to Opus, implementer to Sonnet or an
  explicitly selected second implementer, verifier to GLM, QA to GPT Sol,
  advisor to Argon, judge to Fable, and mechanical to GLM or GLM Flash.
  Implementer and verifier must remain different families.
- Efforts (pinned): `low`, `medium`, `high`, `max`.
  `resolve(verifier, X) = max` and `resolve(qa, X) = max` for every X;
  mechanical keeps its requested effort. Every dispatch carries role AND
  resolved `model:effort`; missing fields error instead of inheriting.
- Verifier provider recovery (pinned): retryable rate-limit/connection cuts
  follow B0.3's bounded resume. Provider-unavailable balance errors instead
  select an authorized alternate BY COMPLEXITY, never below the card's
  declared tier and never in the implementer's family. A different model is
  a FRESH labelled handoff, never resume-with-override. Original reports
  are preserved; completed evidence carries provenance, incomplete or
  suspect evidence is independently measured, and admission caps still bind.
- One registry: the control surface derives its defaults from this registry;
  no second literal role table may drift beside it.
- Guidance: "Never Ask a Model If It's Right" (independence by assignment);
  "The Card Is the Contract" (the current role split and provider recovery).

## Doctrine
- Principles engaged: the router holds the ceiling; one role registry;
  independence by assignment; a model switch is a new evidence-bearing
  handoff, not a convenient resume.
- Why this validation is sufficient: dropped pins, duplicate registries,
  inheritance, collapsed roles and dishonest provider switches each have a
  killing mutant.

## DESIGN SELF-CHECK
1. Claims and pins: every role, pin and recovery rule maps to guarantee
   (a)-(f); no claim makes advisor a verdict authority or QA a verifier.
2. Constants: role names, effort names and recovery seats live in the single
   registry/configuration; tests never re-type a hidden constant.
3. Shared state and test order: resolver fixtures are isolated; recovery
   tests explicitly order ENDED confirmation before fresh handoff.
4. Inherited environment: every recovery fixture starts with a clean
   environment and verifies admission/cap inputs rather than inheriting them.
5. Mutant kills: M1-M5 name their tests; M1 exercises verifier and QA
   separately so one pin cannot mask the other.
6. Composition: B0.3 owns generic resume; this card owns provider-switch
   policy; B0.4 owns profiles; B2.2/B2.3/B3.1 consume the one registry.
